Why your AC is blowing warm air and how to fix it

Discover common reasons your AC might blow warm air and practical steps to resolve the issue in Murrieta and Temecula.

By Steve’s Team

There's nothing worse than expecting a blast of cool air from your AC on a sweltering Murrieta afternoon, only to be met with a warm breeze. If your AC is blowing warm air, it can be frustrating, but don't worry—there are common reasons for this and solutions you can try.

Quick Answer

Your AC might blow warm air due to thermostat issues, dirty filters, or refrigerant leaks. Check these first to solve the problem.

Check Your Thermostat Settings

Before diving into more complex solutions, ensure your thermostat is set correctly. It should be on 'cool' mode and the temperature lower than the current room temperature. Mistakes happen, especially if multiple people use the thermostat.

Inspect and Replace Air Filters

Dirty air filters can restrict airflow, causing your AC to work harder and sometimes blow warm air. Check your filters monthly, especially during the hot summer months in Temecula, and replace them if they're dirty.

Examine the Outdoor Unit

Your AC's outdoor unit might be obstructed by debris or plants. Make sure it's clear and clean. A blocked unit can lead to overheating and inefficiency.

Check for Refrigerant Leaks

Low refrigerant levels due to leaks can cause your AC to blow warm air. This issue requires professional help, so request a quote if you suspect a leak.

Inspect the Circuit Breaker

If the circuit breaker has tripped, your AC might not be receiving power. Check your home's breaker panel and reset any tripped breakers.

When to Call a Professional

If you've tried these steps and your AC still isn't cooling, it might be time to call in the pros. Our team at Steve's Air Conditioning & Heating is ready to help diagnose and fix the issue. Send Steve a photo for a quick diagnosis.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my AC blowing warm air after a power outage?

A power outage can cause your AC to reset. Check your thermostat settings and reset the circuit breaker if necessary.

Can a dirty evaporator coil cause warm air?

Yes, a dirty evaporator coil can limit heat exchange, causing warm air. Cleaning the coil can help resolve this.

How often should I replace my AC filters?

Replace your AC filters every 1-3 months, especially during peak usage periods in summer.
