Common capacitor problems and replacement in AC units

Learn about common capacitor problems in AC units and how to replace them for efficient cooling in Murrieta and Temecula homes.

By Steve’s Team

When your air conditioner isn't cooling like it should on those 100°F+ summer afternoons in Murrieta or Temecula, a faulty capacitor might be to blame. Capacitors are small but mighty components that help start and run your AC unit's motors. Without them, your system can't function properly.

Quick Answer

Common capacitor problems in AC units include failure to start, humming noises, and frequent cycling. Replacing a faulty capacitor can restore your AC's efficiency.

What is a capacitor in an AC unit?

A capacitor is an electrical component that stores and releases energy to start and run the motors in your air conditioning system. There are typically two types: start capacitors and run capacitors. Start capacitors give the motor a boost to start, while run capacitors provide a steady flow of energy to keep the motor running.

Signs of a bad capacitor

Here are some common signs that your AC's capacitor might be failing:

  • Failure to start: If your AC unit struggles to start or doesn't start at all, a bad capacitor could be the cause.
  • Humming noise: A humming noise from your AC unit might indicate the capacitor is trying to work but can't.
  • Frequent cycling: If your AC unit turns on and off more frequently than usual, the capacitor might not be holding a charge.
  • Burning smell: A burning smell can indicate a capacitor failure or other electrical issue.

How to replace a capacitor

Replacing a capacitor involves working with electrical components, so if you're not comfortable doing it yourself, it's best to call a professional. Here's a basic overview of the process:

  1. Turn off power: Ensure the power to the AC unit is completely off.
  2. Locate the capacitor: It's usually found in the outdoor unit, connected to the compressor and fan motor.
  3. Discharge the capacitor: Use an insulated screwdriver to discharge any stored energy.
  4. Remove the old capacitor: Disconnect the wires and remove the old capacitor.
  5. Install the new capacitor: Connect the wires to the new capacitor, ensuring they are in the correct terminals.
  6. Secure the capacitor: Mount the new capacitor in place and restore power to the unit.

When to call a professional

If you're unsure about handling electrical components or if the problem persists after replacing the capacitor, it's time to call a professional. Preventing capacitor issues

Regular maintenance can help prevent capacitor problems. Here are a few tips:

  • Schedule regular maintenance: Regular check-ups can catch capacitor issues before they become major problems. Explore our maintenance services.
  • Keep the unit clean: Dust and debris can cause the capacitor to overheat and fail.
  • Monitor your AC's performance: If you notice any of the signs mentioned earlier, address them promptly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes a capacitor to fail?

Capacitors can fail due to age, overheating, or electrical surges. Regular maintenance can help prevent premature failure.

Can I replace a capacitor myself?

While it's possible, replacing a capacitor involves handling electrical components, so it's often best left to professionals.

How long do AC capacitors last?

AC capacitors typically last around 10-15 years, but this can vary based on usage and maintenance.

Is a humming noise always a sign of a bad capacitor?

Not always, but it can be. A humming noise might also indicate other issues, so it's best to have it checked.
