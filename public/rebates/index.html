HVAC Rebates & Tax Credits

What's currently available for Murrieta, Temecula, and Southwest Riverside County homeowners — and what we'll check for your specific home when we quote your job.

✓
Last verified: May 23, 2026. Rebate programs change throughout the year — funding runs out, new ones open, federal credits change with Congress. We re-verify current program status before every quote so you get accurate numbers, not what was true six months ago.

The honest picture for 2026

The HVAC rebate landscape changed dramatically at the start of 2026. The federal tax credit homeowners were used to (Section 25C) expired December 31, 2025. California's biggest income-qualified rebate program (HEEHRA / TECH Clean California) ran out of single-family funding in February 2026 and is currently waitlist-only. What's still active is a mix of utility-specific rebates from SoCalGas and SCE, statewide instant-rebate coupons at participating retailers, and a state-backed financing program. We walk through each below — with direct links to the official program pages so the dollar amounts you see are always current.

✓ Currently Active

Programs you can actually use right now

These programs are accepting applications as of May 23, 2026. Dollar amounts vary by equipment, household, and program funding cycle — the official-source link on each card always shows current rates.

Active

SoCalGas Home Energy Efficiency Rebates

SoCalGas customers (most of SW Riverside County)

Rebates for ENERGY STAR-certified natural gas equipment installed in existing homes: high-efficiency furnaces (AFUE ≥ 92%), tankless and storage water heaters, dryers, ovens, pool heaters. Available statewide for SoCalGas customers through Dec 31, 2026, or until program funds are exhausted.

What qualifies / who's eligible
  • ENERGY STAR central natural gas furnaces (AFUE ≥ 92%)
  • High-efficiency tankless water heaters
  • High-efficiency storage water heaters (UEF ≥ 0.64, ≤ 55 gal)
  • ENERGY STAR clothes dryers, ovens, fireplace inserts, pool heaters
SoCalGas official rebate page  →
Active

SCE Building Electrification Program

SCE residential electric customers in single-family homes (or 4-unit-or-fewer buildings with individual meters)

Incentives for SCE customers replacing existing gas/propane furnace AND gas/propane water heater with high-efficiency heat pump equipment. Designed to encourage full electrification rather than partial upgrades.

What qualifies / who's eligible
  • Heat pump HVAC replacing existing gas/propane furnace
  • Heat pump water heater replacing existing gas/propane unit
  • Must be an SCE residential electric customer with active individually metered account
  • Home must currently have qualifying gas or propane equipment to replace
The Switch Is On — SCE program page  →
Active

Comfortably CA / Golden State Rebates

All California utility customers (SCE, SoCalGas, PG&E, SDG&E)

Instant rebate coupons applied at participating retailers (in-store or online) on qualifying air conditioners, smart thermostats, electric heat pump water heaters, and gas water heaters. No paperwork — discount is taken off at checkout. Funded by all four California investor-owned utilities under CPUC.

What qualifies / who's eligible
  • Qualifying central and room air conditioners
  • ENERGY STAR smart thermostats
  • Electric heat pump water heaters
  • Gas storage and tankless water heaters
SCE Marketplace rebate hub  →
Active

GoGreen Home Energy Financing

California homeowners — not a rebate, but a state-backed financing program

Below-market interest rate loans for energy-efficient home upgrades, administered by the California State Treasurer's Office (CAEATFA). No collateral required, FICO starting as low as 580. State backs the loan loss reserve so participating lenders (mainly credit unions) can offer reduced rates and flexible terms.

What qualifies / who's eligible
  • Qualifying HVAC upgrades (heat pumps, furnaces, AC)
  • Water heater upgrades
  • Windows, insulation, lighting, and other eligible energy measures
  • Loan amounts typically $2,500 – $75,000, terms 1-20 years

Worth knowing about, but not currently funding new projects

This program previously offered significant rebates but exhausted its 2026 funding. New applications go on a waitlist with no funding guarantee. We still mention it here so you know what it is and can monitor for Phase II reopening.

HEEHRA / TECH Clean California (Single-Family)

Income-qualified California homeowners (single-family) — currently waitlist-only

California's largest heat-pump rebate program, funded by the federal Inflation Reduction Act and administered by the California Energy Commission through TECH Clean California. As of February 24, 2026, single-family rebates are fully reserved statewide and the program is no longer accepting new income verification applications. New reservation requests go on a waitlist and are only funded if additional money becomes available. Multifamily projects are still being funded through a separate budget.

What qualifies / who's eligible
  • Income-qualified single-family homeowners (low- and moderate-income tiers)
  • Heat pump HVAC system replacing an existing primary heating source
  • Must use a HEEHRA-trained, TECH Clean California-certified contractor
  • Equipment must meet ENERGY STAR certification and 2026 EPA refrigerant rules (GWP ≤ 700)

What used to be available — and why it isn't anymore

You may have seen these programs advertised on older HVAC websites, in last year's tax filings, or on competitor pages that haven't been updated. Here's the straight answer on what happened.

Expired (Dec 31, 2025)

Federal 25C Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit

U.S. homeowners — no longer available for 2026 installs

A federal tax credit that covered 30% of qualifying energy-efficient home improvements (up to $3,200/year, with heat pumps capped at $2,000 and AC/furnace at $600). Was originally scheduled to run through 2032 but terminated by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119-21) for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. Equipment installed in 2025 is still claimable on your 2025 federal return.

What qualifies / who's eligible
  • Equipment placed in service on or before December 31, 2025: still claimable on 2025 federal return (IRS Form 5695)
  • Equipment placed in service January 1, 2026 or later: not eligible
  • Watch this space — pending Democratic legislation would restore the credit, but as of mid-2026 no restoration is law

How rebates actually work

What homeowners should know before they bank on a rebate

1

Equipment has to qualify

Every rebate program publishes a list of eligible models, efficiency ratings (SEER2, HSPF2, AFUE, UEF), and brand certifications. Buying the wrong model — even a similar one — can disqualify you. We specify qualifying equipment by default on rebate-relevant quotes.

2

Some are instant, some take 6–8 weeks

Comfortably CA / Golden State Rebates are instant — the discount comes off at checkout. SoCalGas and most utility rebates are mail-in, typically 6–8 weeks to receive a prepaid card. HEEHRA (when it was active) required pre-approval before installation. Knowing which is which matters when you're cash-flowing the job.

3

Stacking is sometimes allowed

You can often combine a utility rebate (e.g. SoCalGas) with a state program (e.g. Comfortably CA) with manufacturer instant rebates. But some programs explicitly disallow stacking with others — the terms vary by program. We check stacking eligibility when we quote your job.

4

Funding runs out mid-year

HEEHRA ran out statewide on February 24, 2026 — less than two months into the program year. Federal 25C expired entirely. The lesson: don't plan a project around a specific rebate unless you can submit a reservation before installation. Active programs today may not be active tomorrow.

Common questions

Rebate FAQ for SW Riverside County homeowners

Is the federal HVAC tax credit available in 2026?

No. The Section 25C Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (which provided up to $3,200/year for qualifying energy-efficient improvements, including heat pumps and high-efficiency HVAC) expired December 31, 2025, terminated by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Equipment placed in service on or before that date is still claimable on your 2025 federal return using IRS Form 5695. Equipment installed January 1, 2026 or later is not eligible.

Democratic legislators have introduced bills to restore the credit, but as of May 23, 2026 no restoration has been signed into law. We'll update this page if that changes.

What is HEEHRA and can I still get the $8,000 heat pump rebate?

HEEHRA (Home Energy Rebates) is the federal Inflation Reduction Act program administered in California by the California Energy Commission through TECH Clean California. It offered up to $8,000 for low-income households and $4,000 for moderate-income households installing a qualifying heat pump HVAC system.

As of February 24, 2026, single-family HEEHRA funding is fully reserved statewide. The program is no longer accepting new income verification applications for single-family projects. New reservation requests are placed on a waitlist and only funded if additional money becomes available. A Phase II is planned but no timeline has been confirmed. Multifamily projects continue to have funding available through a separate budget.

The best place to check current status is the TECH Clean California HEEHRA page.

I'm a SoCalGas customer in Murrieta. What rebates can I actually use?

The most accessible program for you is the SoCalGas Home Energy Efficiency Rebate Program, which offers rebates on ENERGY STAR-certified natural gas furnaces (AFUE ≥ 92%), high-efficiency tankless and storage water heaters, dryers, ovens, and fireplace inserts. The program is active through December 31, 2026, or until funding is exhausted. Current dollar amounts and the full eligible-equipment list are on the official SoCalGas rebate page.

You can also use Comfortably CA / Golden State Rebates for instant rebates on water heaters and smart thermostats at participating retailers.

I'm an SCE customer. What about heat pump rebates?

SCE's Building Electrification Program offers incentives for SCE residential electric customers replacing existing gas or propane furnace AND water heater equipment with high-efficiency heat pumps. The program targets full home electrification rather than partial upgrades — you typically need to replace both the heating system and the water heater. Eligibility requires being an SCE residential electric customer with an active individually metered account. Current rebate amounts are on The Switch Is On program profile.

Can rebates be combined / stacked?

Often yes — for example, a SoCalGas furnace rebate stacks with manufacturer instant rebates and (when active) federal tax credits. Some programs explicitly disallow stacking with specific others; for example, SCE's Home Performance Plus program cannot be combined with other programs funded by the same California Utilities Public Purpose Fund. Stacking rules are program-specific and listed in each program's terms.

We check stacking eligibility when we quote a job that touches multiple rebate-eligible upgrades.

Does Steve's file the rebate paperwork for me?

The applications themselves are the homeowner's responsibility — the utility wants your name and account on the form. We provide everything you need to submit them: itemized invoice with model numbers, efficiency certifications, ENERGY STAR documentation, and permit closure documentation where applicable. For pre-approval programs (like HEEHRA when it's active), we'll help you submit the reservation request before installation begins.

What about financing if rebates aren't enough?

GoGreen Home Energy Financing is a state-backed program administered by the California State Treasurer's Office (CAEATFA) that provides below-market interest loans for energy upgrades. No collateral required, FICO starting as low as 580, loan amounts from $2,500 to $75,000, terms up to 20 years. The state backs the loan loss reserve so participating credit unions can offer reduced rates. More details and current rates at the official GoGreen Home site.

Why doesn't this page list specific dollar amounts?

Because rebate amounts change throughout the year — sometimes within weeks. Programs run out of funding mid-year (HEEHRA exhausted statewide single-family funding before March 2026), federal credits get terminated by Congress (Section 25C ended December 31, 2025), and equipment efficiency thresholds get updated annually. Any hardcoded number we put on this page would be stale within months.

Instead, we link directly to each program's official page so the dollar amount you see is always the current one. And when we quote your specific job, we verify current program status against your equipment, your utility, and your eligibility — then tell you a realistic figure for your install.
