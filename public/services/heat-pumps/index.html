Heat Pumps

Year-round heating and cooling efficiency

Heat pumps are one of the most energy-efficient ways to heat and cool your home — and the mild Southern California climate is just about the perfect environment for them. A single heat pump system replaces both your AC and your furnace, moving heat instead of generating it, which means dramatically lower energy bills year-round. For Murrieta and Temecula homes that run AC nine months out of twelve, the math often works out heavily in favor of a heat pump.

Steve's installs and services air-source heat pumps for residential and light commercial applications, plus dual-fuel systems that pair a heat pump with a backup gas furnace for those rare cold snaps. With the federal Inflation Reduction Act tax credit ($2,000 for qualifying heat pumps) plus California rebates, the upfront cost gap between a heat pump and a traditional AC + furnace setup is smaller than it's been in years. We'll walk you through the real numbers for your situation.

How It Works

Our Heat Pump Process

Maximum efficiency, full rebate guidance, honest install.

1

Home Assessment & Heat Load

We measure your home, check your existing system, electrical panel, and ductwork (or lack of it), and run a load calculation. Heat pumps are sized differently than gas furnaces — getting it right matters for both efficiency and comfort year-round.

2

System Selection & Rebate Review

We help you choose between an air-source heat pump or dual-fuel system based on your home's needs. We walk you through federal tax credits, SCE/SDG&E rebates, and any utility incentives that apply — getting you the full discount you qualify for, not just the easy ones.

3

Professional Installation

We set the outdoor unit on a proper pad with vibration isolation, install the indoor air handler or tie into existing ductwork, route the lineset cleanly, and handle all electrical and refrigerant work. Done correctly the first time so it actually performs like a heat pump should.

4

Startup, Test & Walkthrough

We charge the system, test heating and cooling modes, and verify performance in both. Then we walk you through the controls, defrost behavior in winter, and what to expect from heat pump operation — which behaves a little differently than what most people are used to.

Common Questions

Heat Pumps FAQs

Straight answers about heat pumps in the Murrieta-Temecula Valley. Don't see your question? Call (951) 634-3233.

How much does a heat pump cost installed in Temecula?
A standard residential heat pump installation in our area typically runs $7,000 to $15,000 depending on system size, efficiency rating, and whether ductwork modifications are needed. After federal tax credits and California rebates, the net cost can drop significantly. We'll give you a clear written quote with all incentives factored in.
What's the difference between a heat pump and an AC + furnace?
A traditional setup uses two separate systems: an AC for cooling and a gas furnace for heating. A heat pump is a single system that does both — it moves heat outside in summer and pulls heat inside in winter. One unit, no gas line needed (for all-electric installs), and dramatically more energy-efficient than electric resistance heating.
Are heat pumps efficient in Southern California?
Yes — in fact, this climate is ideal for them. Heat pumps lose efficiency in extreme cold (below ~30°F), but the Murrieta-Temecula Valley rarely sees those temperatures. Our long cooling season means the heat pump runs efficiently 9-10 months a year, and on the coldest nights, modern heat pumps still perform well or pair with a small backup.
What rebates and tax credits are available for heat pumps?
The federal Inflation Reduction Act offers a $2,000 tax credit for qualifying heat pump installations. California's TECH Clean California program offers heat pump rebates, though some Southern California reservations are currently waitlisted — we'll check current availability for your situation. SCE may also offer additional incentives. Call (951) 634-3233 and we'll lay out exactly what you qualify for.
How long does a heat pump last?
Modern air-source heat pumps typically last 12-15 years with proper maintenance. That's somewhat shorter than a gas furnace (which can last 20+ years), but the energy savings over the system's life usually more than make up the difference, especially in our climate.
Should I switch from a gas furnace to a heat pump?
If your existing AC and furnace are both aging (10+ years), it's a great time to consider switching. You replace two systems with one, qualify for federal tax credits and state rebates, and lock in lower energy bills going forward. If only one system is aging, the math is closer — we'll run real numbers for your specific situation.
What's a dual-fuel heat pump system?
A dual-fuel setup pairs a heat pump (for everyday heating and cooling) with a smaller backup gas furnace that kicks in only on the coldest nights. You get the efficiency of a heat pump for 95% of the year, plus the brute heating power of gas for the rare 30°F nights in the valley. Best of both worlds for homeowners who want maximum reliability.
Do you give free estimates on heat pump installation?
Yes — free in-home estimates on all heat pump installations, including a full breakdown of available federal tax credits and California rebates. Call (951) 634-3233 to schedule.
