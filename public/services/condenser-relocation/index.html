Condenser Relocation

Safe, code-compliant relocation of outdoor AC & heat pump units

Sometimes the outdoor unit just isn't where it should be. Maybe a remodel or an addition put it in the way. Maybe it's crowding a walkway, blocking a gate, or sitting right outside a bedroom window where the noise is a problem. Maybe new landscaping or a patio project needs the space, or the unit is jammed too close to a wall and can't get the airflow it needs to run efficiently. Whatever the reason, relocating a condenser is a real job — it's not just unbolting it and carrying it across the yard. It involves recovering the refrigerant, disconnecting the electrical, rerouting or extending the refrigerant line set and control wiring, setting a new level pad, and confirming the new spot meets clearance and code requirements.

Done wrong, a condenser move can damage the compressor, kink the line set, leave the unit unlevel, or put it somewhere that strangles its airflow — and you'll feel all of that in performance and lifespan. We handle the whole process: we evaluate the new location for clearance, airflow, drainage, and code; recover the refrigerant properly; move and reset the unit on a solid level pad; reroute the line set and electrical cleanly; recharge and pressure-test the system; and verify it runs the way it should before we leave. A relocation is also a good moment to catch wear on the line set or electrical that's worth addressing while everything's open.

How It Works

Our Relocation Process

Careful planning and clean workmanship — so the move protects your system.

1

On-Site Evaluation & Relocation Plan

We inspect the current setup — unit, line set, electrical, pad, drainage — and evaluate the new location for clearance, airflow, drainage, and code. Then we give you a clear plan and a written, itemized quote.

2

Refrigerant Recovery & Disconnection

On service day we safely recover the refrigerant, disconnect the power, and prepare the line set and wiring for the move. Doing this step properly is what protects the compressor and the system.

3

Move, Reset & Reconnection

We relocate the unit, set it on a solid level pad, and reroute or extend the line set, control wiring, and electrical to the new location with clean workmanship — no kinked lines, no shortcuts.

4

Recharge, Pressure-Test & Verify

We recharge the system, pressure-test for leaks, check airflow and amp draw, and confirm the unit runs properly in its new spot before we walk you through the finished setup.

Common Questions

Condenser Relocation FAQs

Straight answers about condenser relocation in the Murrieta-Temecula Valley. Don't see your question? Call (951) 634-3233.

Why would I need to relocate my AC condenser?
The most common reasons are remodels and additions that put the unit in the way, landscaping or patio projects that need the space, noise (the unit is too close to a bedroom or patio), poor clearance (it's jammed against a wall and can't breathe), and improving access or curb appeal. Whatever the reason, the move needs to be done properly to protect the system.
Can moving my outdoor unit damage my AC?
It can, if it's done carelessly — a botched move can damage the compressor, kink the refrigerant line set, leave the unit unlevel, or place it where airflow is restricted. Done correctly, with proper refrigerant recovery, clean line-set routing, a level pad, and a real clearance evaluation, a relocation protects your system rather than harming it.
How do you decide where the condenser should go?
We look at clearance from walls and obstructions, airflow space around the unit, drainage, noise relative to windows and patios, electrical access, line-set routing distance, and local code requirements. The new spot has to let the unit breathe and run efficiently — not just be out of the way.
Is condenser relocation a big job?
It's more involved than people expect. It includes refrigerant recovery, electrical disconnection, line-set rerouting or extension, a new level pad, reconnection, recharging, and pressure-testing. It's not a quick lift-and-carry. That said, for a straightforward move it's typically completed efficiently once we're on site — we'll give you a timeline with your quote.
Will I need a new pad for the unit?
Usually, yes. The condenser needs to sit on a solid, level surface in its new location — an unlevel unit causes vibration, noise, and premature wear. Setting a proper new pad is a standard part of the relocation.
Can you relocate a heat pump, not just an AC?
Yes — the process is essentially the same for a heat pump's outdoor unit. We evaluate the new location, recover refrigerant, reroute the line set and electrical, set a level pad, and verify operation. We service and relocate both AC condensers and heat pump units.
Should I fix anything else while the unit is being moved?
A relocation is a good opportunity to address worn line-set insulation, corroded fittings, or aging electrical disconnects, since much of it is already exposed during the move. We'll point out anything we see that's worth handling while we're there — but we won't pad the job with work you don't need.
Do you offer free estimates on condenser relocation?
Yes — free estimates on condenser relocation projects. We evaluate your current setup and the proposed new location, then give you a written, itemized quote. Call (951) 634-3233 to schedule your free assessment.
